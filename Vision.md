# Core Vision

A living space simulation where the player exists inside a large universe that continues changing independently of them.

The player is not the center of the galaxy.

The universe contains:

* factions
* politics
* trade
* economies
* conflict
* infrastructure
* people
* changing information
* long-term events

The player begins small and grows into a larger role inside that universe without ever completely controlling it.

The game is primarily a sandbox.

The universe should feel alive because things are actually happening.

---

# Universe View

The main screen is primarily a **galaxy / universe view**.

The player sees many solar systems spread across space.

The player can:

* select systems
* zoom into systems
* move between map layers

---

# Solar System View

Zooming into a system reveals a more detailed local view.

Possible visible elements:

* planets
* stations
* asteroid fields
* ships *(depending on visibility rules)*

---

# Fog of War / Information Philosophy

The player does not possess perfect information.

Information depends on presence, assets, crew, and intelligence.

If the player or trusted crew are present in a system:

high-detail awareness is available.

If the player lacks presence:

information becomes limited or outdated.

Static knowledge may remain.

Dynamic reality may not.

Examples:

The player may know a planet exists.

The player may not know:

* faction ownership changed
* a station was destroyed
* pirate activity increased
* trade collapsed
* military occupation occurred

Player knowledge and actual reality are separate concepts.

---

# Living Universe Simulation

The universe continues changing in real time.

However, not every location requires full high-detail simulation all the time.

Systems with player presence, crew presence, or active assets may run in higher detail.

Other systems may run through more abstract background simulation.

Systems can catch up when player awareness returns.

The player should feel:

*"things happened while I was away."*

---

# Factions and Galaxy Structure

The galaxy contains many solar systems.

Major factions possess home systems.

Faction home systems are separated by buffer regions. Buffers must be at least 3 jumps. 

To travel to another faction player must pass though at least one lawless zone. 

Home systems tend to be safer.

Outer systems become increasingly uncertain and dangerous.

Reputation matters.

Faction territory safety depends partly on how factions view the player.

The universe contains ongoing political activity, conflict, and large-scale events.

The project uses original factions and lore.

Inspirational influences are not copied directly.

---

# Travel Philosophy

Interstellar movement relies heavily on a jump gate network.

Most ships depend on existing gates.

Advanced or extremely capable ships may eventually support independent jump capability.

Travel matters.

Distance matters.

Moving between safe major regions often requires multiple jumps through less secure territory.

---

# Economy Philosophy

The economy should not feel magical.

Goods should exist because someone mined, refined, manufactured, transported, traded, or consumed them.

Markets are connected to the living universe.

Cities, stations, colonies, and planets consume resources.

Production chains matter.

Trade routes matter.

Shortages matter.

Logistics matter.

NPC ships should generally have actual purposes.

Not decorative traffic.

Examples:

* mining
* hauling
* patrol
* escort
* industrial work
* trade operations

Mission opportunities can emerge naturally from real economic conditions.

---

# Player Philosophy

The player begins as an ordinary person with limited resources.

No chosen hero.

No galaxy ruler.

The player starts with only enough money to begin space life.

Possibly through:

* inheritance
* savings
* starting funds

The player chooses a starter ship.

The player gradually builds:

* money
* experience
* crew
* ships
* influence
* infrastructure

The player remains a real physical person inside the universe.

No magical teleportation between owned assets.

---

# Crew and Characters

Crew are people.

Not disposable stat blocks.

Crew can possess:

* skills
* traits
* personalities
* strengths
* weaknesses

Possible traits:

* hardworking
* lazy
* night owl
* early riser
* friendly
* loner

Crew matter operationally.

Crew quality affects outcomes.

People performing work influence how effectively systems actually operate.

---

# Player Character

The player character uses character creation.

Backstories influence starting identity.

Backstories affect:

* skills
* traits
* strengths
* drawbacks

Tradeoffs matter.

The player can personally participate in ship operations while still acting as leader and commander.

---

# Crew Recruitment

Crew can be acquired through different paths.

Examples:

* hiring boards
* paid employment
* missions
* personal loyalty
* friendships
* rescued survivors
* earned relationships

The game may include a small introductory story/tutorial path that helps establish an initial crew and early direction before opening into the wider sandbox.

---

# Ships and Components

Ships use a component-based philosophy.

Ships possess identities and intended strengths.

Customization exists.

Ships are not completely fixed.

Component slots may include:

* dedicated slots
* flexible slots
* different size categories

Ship scale matters.

Larger ships support more systems, larger equipment, and broader operational capability.

Crew requirements increase with ship complexity.

Large ships become genuine multi-crew operations.

---

# Skills, Experience, and Growth

Both players and crew can improve over time.

Skills are not completely static.

Experience matters.

Growth can occur through:

* work
* service
* survival
* training
* continued operation

Crew development matters.

A rookie may become a trusted veteran.

---

# Long-Term Progression

The game is a sandbox without a traditional ending.

Late progression may include:

* multiple ships
* trusted crews
* remote operations
* small infrastructure
* player-owned station(s)
* modest prosperity
* meaningful regional influence

The intended fantasy is successful independence and operational growth.

Not galactic domination.

"Penthouse with a view, not owner of the entire building."
